PART 9 — LEADERSHIP, COLLABORATION & ROLE FIT

43. WHY MOVE FROM SENIOR ENGINEERING MANAGEMENT TO AN INDIVIDUAL CONTRIBUTOR PRODUCT ROLE?

Likelihood: EXTREMELY HIGH | 60 seconds

For me, this is about the opportunity and the work, not the management title.

Throughout my career, I’ve been most energized by understanding business problems, designing solutions, and delivering technology that improves how employees work.

At Workday, I’ve owned product strategy, roadmap prioritization, architecture, and delivery for AI-enabled workflows and enterprise platforms.

While I’ve managed engineering organizations, I’ve remained actively involved in technical decisions, product direction, stakeholder collaboration, and operational execution.

What excites me about Nscale is the opportunity to help build a People Technology function from the ground up.

I wouldn’t be stepping away from leadership.

I’d be applying that leadership through product ownership, technical execution, stakeholder influence, and measurable business outcomes.

Austin’s vision of combining Workday expertise, application engineering, and AI-native HR experiences aligns closely with where I want to focus my career.

KEY MESSAGE: I’m choosing the opportunity to build and own meaningful products, not optimizing for management hierarchy.

44. HOW DO YOU WORK WITH HR STAKEHOLDERS WHO ARE NOT TECHNICAL?

Likelihood: VERY HIGH | 60 seconds

I start by understanding the business problem, rather than immediately discussing technology.

For example, if Talent Acquisition says onboarding is taking too long, I wouldn’t begin by recommending a new integration or automation tool.

I’d ask questions about where delays occur, which teams are involved, what manual work exists, and how success is measured.

Then I’d map the current workflow and identify opportunities for improvement.

When presenting solutions, I’d explain the options in terms of business impact, employee experience, implementation effort, cost, and operational risk.

I avoid unnecessary technical terminology and focus on helping stakeholders understand the trade-offs.

At Workday, I regularly partner with People Operations, Talent, Finance, IT, Legal, and Security to translate operational needs into product and engineering priorities.

KEY MESSAGE: Translate technical complexity into clear business decisions that stakeholders can understand and support.

45. TELL ME ABOUT A TIME YOU HAD TO INFLUENCE STAKEHOLDERS WITHOUT DIRECT AUTHORITY.

Likelihood: HIGH | 60–90 seconds

At Workday, I led product prioritization for a shared AI platform supporting more than 300 engineering teams.

Different teams had competing requirements, and we couldn’t implement everything simultaneously.

My responsibility was to establish a common approach to prioritization, governance, and platform adoption.

I worked with engineering leaders, product teams, security, and other stakeholders to understand their requirements and identify common needs.

Rather than allowing every team to develop separate solutions, we established reusable platform capabilities, shared standards, and evaluation frameworks.

We also created documentation, office hours, and adoption support to help teams transition.

As a result, we improved evaluated agent task success from 15% to 65% across a measured cohort of 24 teams.
