UNDERSTANDING AUSTIN PENNA

This is where your preparation becomes especially important.

Austin isn’t a traditional HR leader.

He’s a technical People Systems leader with deep Workday implementation experience.

Based on the background you provided, he has:

Approximately 15 years of People Technology experience.
Extensive Workday implementation and consulting experience.
Experience owning Workday at Meta.
Hands-on exposure to HCM, Payroll, Compensation, Time Tracking, Recruiting, Benefits, and Extend.
Experience leading functional and integration teams.
Experience managing large-scale People Technology portfolios.

He also holds a Workday PRO: HCM certification.

Why this matters

Austin will likely be comfortable discussing Workday at multiple levels:

Business level: How should an employee workflow operate?

Product level: What should we prioritize?

Configuration level: How should Workday be configured?

Architecture level: How should Workday integrate with adjacent systems?

Engineering level: What should be built outside Workday?

AI level: How could an agent improve the experience?

You need to demonstrate competence across all six.

The Most Important Thing Austin Said

In his LinkedIn announcement, he asked:

“If we did it all over again from a blank page, how would we do it differently?”

Then:

“Rather than automating that legacy workflow, or bolting some AI feature onto it, how could we build an AI-native HR function from the ground up?”

This is the central theme of your interview.

Austin doesn’t simply want someone who can maintain Workday.

He wants someone who can help him rethink how People Technology should operate.

But he also recognizes that foundational Workday architecture must be correct.

His other important statement was:

“Foundations are cheaper to pour at this size and costly to retrofit later.”

That tells me he cares about:

Getting foundational architecture right.
Establishing scalable business processes.
Creating consistent data governance.
Designing extensible integrations.
Building AI capabilities into the architecture.
Avoiding expensive future rework.

Your goal is to convince Austin that you understand both enterprise-scale People Systems and startup-speed execution.
