NSCALE — INTERVIEW PREPARATION

Austin Penna | Director, People Technology

1. What Austin Is Likely Looking For

Based on his experience and the job description, I believe Austin will evaluate you across six dimensions.

￼

Austin’s background suggests he may ask detailed follow-up questions rather than accepting high-level answers.

For example, if you say:

“I would configure a conditional approval in Workday.”

He may follow up with:

“Okay, walk me through how you would actually configure that in the tenant.”

That’s the level of preparation I want you to reach.

⸻

2. How I Would Change Your Study Priorities

Given Austin’s background, I would adjust your preparation allocation.

￼

I would keep the same overall allocation, but increase the technical depth within each category.

The biggest change is that I would prepare you to answer configuration-level questions, not just conceptual questions.

⸻

3. The 10 Questions Austin Is Most Likely to Ask

These are my predictions based on his experience, not confirmed interview questions.

QUESTION 1 — WORKDAY EXPERIENCE

“Tell me about your hands-on Workday configuration experience.”

Why he’ll ask: He spent years configuring and implementing Workday and knows what direct administration entails.

Your approach: Clearly distinguish between your experience working at Workday and any customer-side configuration you have personally performed.

Emphasize your HCM domain expertise, system architecture, integrations, automation, and delivery experience without claiming tenant configuration experience you haven’t established.

QUESTION 2 — CORE HCM

“Walk me through how you would configure a new supervisory organization.”

Prepare to explain:

Supervisory organization hierarchy.
Manager assignments.
Staffing model.
Organization roles.
Security implications.
Hiring restrictions.
Testing and validation.

QUESTION 3 — BUSINESS PROCESSES

“How would you configure a conditional approval in Workday?”

Prepare to explain:

Business process definition.
Approval step.
Condition rule.
Approver security group or role.
Effective configuration.
Testing.
Troubleshooting.

QUESTION 4 — SECURITY

“What’s the difference between domain security and business process security?”

Prepare to explain:

Domain security: Controls access to secured data and operations.

Business process security: Controls authorized actions within business processes.

Austin may then ask how you would troubleshoot an employee who can view information but cannot initiate or approve a transaction.

QUESTION 5 — INTEGRATIONS

“How would you integrate Greenhouse with Workday?”

Prepare to explain:

System ownership.
Data mapping.
Integration triggers.
API selection.
Authentication.
Error handling.
Reconciliation.
Monitoring.

QUESTION 6 — TROUBLESHOOTING

“An employee’s transfer is stuck in Workday. Walk me through your investigation.”

Prepare to explain:

Business process status.
Current step.
Assigned approver.
Condition rules.
Security.
Effective dates.
Integration dependencies.

QUESTION 7 — GLOBAL SCALING

“How would you design Workday to support Nscale’s expansion into five new countries?”

Prepare to explain:

Legal entities.
Supervisory organizations.
Staffing models.
Local compliance.
Payroll integrations.
Regional security.
Global reporting.

QUESTION 8 — PRODUCT MANAGEMENT

“How would you prioritize the People Systems roadmap?”

Prepare to explain:

Business impact.
Operational risk.
Employee experience.
Compliance.
Implementation effort.
Cost.
Measurable outcomes.

QUESTION 9 — AI

“Where would you introduce AI agents into People Systems?”

This is one of your strongest areas.

Discuss:

Employee self-service.
Integration monitoring.
Data quality.
Release testing.
Human-in-the-loop controls.

QUESTION 10 — INDIVIDUAL CONTRIBUTOR

“You’ve led engineering teams for years. Are you comfortable doing the work yourself?”

This question deserves special attention because the position requires independent execution.

Your response should emphasize your willingness to own problems from investigation through implementation, while being specific about which Workday administration skills you have directly practiced.

⸻

Austin has deep expertise in Workday administration, implementation, HR operations, and product ownership.

Your experience brings another dimension:

Enterprise engineering + HCM systems + automated quality engineering + AI-native platforms.

That’s potentially valuable because Nscale wants to build more than a traditional HRIS administration function.

It wants to develop integrations, automations, agentic workflows, and scalable People Systems infrastructure.

You can bring specific examples involving:

Workday

Led a 12-engineer organization building enterprise AI platforms.
Supported platforms used across hundreds of engineering teams.
Delivered governed agent execution and evaluation capabilities.
Improved release readiness through engineering and quality practices.

Cornerstone

Led engineering teams delivering HCM and talent management solutions.
Worked across recruiting, onboarding, learning, and performance systems.
Managed enterprise integrations and customer-facing delivery.
Led quality and operational improvement initiatives.

EmCentrix

Worked with HCM, payroll, and HR technology.
Supported enterprise delivery, integrations, and quality engineering.

The key is connecting these accomplishments directly to Austin’s priorities.

For example:

Austin’s priority: Workday platform stability.

Your experience: Enterprise test automation, regression testing, release readiness, and operational reliability.

Austin’s priority: Connected People Systems.

Austin’s priority: AI-powered People Technology.

⸻

5. The Most Important Question You Should Ask Austin

I would ask:

“As Nscale continues scaling globally, where do you see the biggest opportunity to improve the People Systems ecosystem today? Is it strengthening the Workday foundation, improving integrations across the employee lifecycle, or introducing more automation and AI capabilities?”

This is an excellent question because it demonstrates that you understand the three major dimensions of the position:

Platform foundation + connected systems + intelligent automation.

It also gives you an opportunity to discuss the area where you could make the greatest contribution.

⸻

6. One Risk I Would Prepare For

Austin’s profile suggests he may have particularly high expectations for hands-on Workday expertise.

He holds Workday Pro: HCM and has extensive experience implementing Workday across multiple functional areas.

Nscale’s job description explicitly calls for someone who can configure Workday independently.

If your direct Workday tenant administration experience is limited, that may be the biggest concern in the interview.

I would prepare a response that acknowledges the distinction while highlighting your transferable expertise.

Suggested Talking Script

“My background combines enterprise HCM technology, engineering leadership, integrations, automation, and AI platform development.

At Workday, I worked on the engineering side, leading teams responsible for enterprise AI platforms and automation capabilities.

Earlier in my career, I worked extensively with HCM, recruiting, onboarding, payroll, learning, and talent management technologies at Cornerstone and EmCentrix.

I’ve been responsible for end-to-end solution delivery, system integrations, quality engineering, automation, and production reliability.

I also recognize that building technology at Workday and administering a customer’s Workday tenant are different disciplines.

My strengths are in understanding complex People Systems, designing scalable solutions, solving technical problems, and driving improvements from concept through delivery.

What particularly interests me about Nscale is the opportunity to combine that experience with hands-on People Systems ownership, while bringing additional capabilities in AI, automation, integration reliability, and operational efficiency.

I’m very comfortable working independently, getting into the technical details, and being accountable for the outcome.”


7. My Recommendation Before Your Interview

I would prepare for Austin as though you were interviewing with a senior Workday solution architect who also owns the People Technology roadmap.

Spend your remaining technical preparation time on three exercises:

Exercise 1 — Configure a Hiring Business Process

Be able to explain how you would configure a hiring workflow with conditional approvals, security requirements, and downstream integrations.

Exercise 2 — Troubleshoot a Security Issue

Be able to explain how you would investigate an HR partner who cannot access compensation data or approve a transaction.

Exercise 3 — Design Greenhouse-to-Workday Integration

Be able to explain the architecture, data mapping, security, error handling, monitoring, and recovery strategy.

If you can walk Austin through these scenarios in technical detail, while also discussing product strategy, global scaling, cost reduction, and AI automation, you’ll be much better prepared for the conversation.

The most important adjustment is this: don’t prepare to impress Austin with Workday terminology. Prepare to demonstrate how you think through real Workday configuration and operational problems.
