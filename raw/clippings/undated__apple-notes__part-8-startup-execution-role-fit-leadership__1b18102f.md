35. BALANCING HANDS-ON EXECUTION & PRODUCT STRATEGY
HOW WOULD YOU BALANCE HANDS-ON WORKDAY ADMINISTRATION, PRODUCT STRATEGY, INTEGRATIONS, AND AI DEVELOPMENT?
Likelihood: EXTREMELY HIGH | 60–75 seconds
KEYWORDS
Hands-On Configuration | Product Roadmap | Integrations | AI Automation | Prioritization | Ownership
TALKING SCRIPT
“I see those responsibilities as connected parts of owning a People Systems product, rather than separate functions.
First, I’d prioritize platform stability, security, and critical employee workflows. Workday needs to be reliable before we introduce additional complexity.
Second, I’d maintain a clear product roadmap aligned with Nscale’s business priorities, working closely with People Operations, Talent Acquisition, Finance, IT, and Security.
Third, I’d remain hands-on with Workday configuration, integrations, troubleshooting, and testing, particularly where those changes directly improve employee workflows.
Fourth, I’d identify opportunities for automation and AI, focusing on high-friction processes where we can deliver measurable value.
At Workday, I balanced product strategy, architecture, engineering execution, stakeholder management, and AI development, while leading a team of 12 engineers.
What attracts me to Nscale is the opportunity to combine strategic ownership with hands-on execution — identifying the problem, designing the solution, and ensuring it delivers results.”
KEY MESSAGE
Platform Stability → Product Roadmap → Hands-On Configuration → Integrations → AI → Measurable Outcomes

TECHNICAL FOLLOW-UP
Austin: “What would you do if you had a critical Workday issue, three roadmap requests, and an AI prototype all competing for your time?”
30–45 SECOND RESPONSE
“I’d prioritize based on business impact, urgency, and risk.
If the Workday issue affects payroll, hiring, employee access, or data security, that becomes the immediate priority.
Next, I’d assess the roadmap requests based on business value, dependencies, and deadlines, and communicate any necessary changes to stakeholders.
The AI prototype would continue only if it supports a critical business objective or can progress without affecting platform reliability.
My philosophy is that production stability and employee-impacting issues come first, followed by the highest-value improvements.”
