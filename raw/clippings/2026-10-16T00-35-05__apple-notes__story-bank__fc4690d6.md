STORY BANK

STORY 1 — OPERATION STABILIZE

Use for: Reliability, portfolio ownership, cross-functional leadership, ambiguity, or operational improvement.

Situation

At Cornerstone, recurring failures were affecting workflows across 21 Talent and HCM products and more than 50 integrations.

The systems had different architectures, data models, owners, and release processes, but customers experienced them as one connected journey.

Task

I needed to identify the systemic causes and create a recovery model that the full organization could execute.

Action

I organized the effort around end-to-end customer workflows and business risk.

We combined:

Support data
Quality data
Delivery information
Usage data
Integration failures

We identified recurring failure patterns and ownership gaps.

I aligned Product, Engineering, Quality, Support, and Operations around:

Recovery priorities
Escalation paths
Integration validation
Release controls
Clear ownership

Result

Within 90 days, we established a clearer operating model, stabilized the highest-risk areas, and created more durable ownership across the portfolio.

Relevance to Nscale

The experience applies directly to Workday, Greenhouse, and downstream People Systems because the employee experiences one workflow, even when several systems and teams are involved.

Allowing every team to recreate retrieval, permissions, evaluation, and operational tooling would have created unnecessary risk and cost.

Improve

STORY 5 — PRIORITIZATION UNDER CONFLICT

Use for: Stakeholder disagreement, roadmap trade-offs, executive communication, or judgment.

Situation

During a Talent product release, business stakeholders requested additional capabilities while Engineering was concerned about capacity and delivery risk.

Task

I needed to reconcile the competing positions while protecting the underlying business commitment.

Action

I investigated the requests and discovered that some were tied to contractual commitments that had not been visible during planning.

I separated contractual commitments from preferences and presented three options:

Expand scope and accept a delay
Protect the original scope
Include the contractual requirements while moving lower-value work to a later release

I recommended the third option—a deliberate scope exchange.

Result

We agreed on a supportable release plan and made contractual commitments an explicit input into future prioritization.

Lesson

Establish the facts, recommend a path, and state clearly what moves when a priority changes.
