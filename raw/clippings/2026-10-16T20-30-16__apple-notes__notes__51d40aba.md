Story 8: Talent Engagement Layer Architecture
Use For
Architecture, AI, Talent Tech strategy, ambiguity, communication, scope.
Situation
At Workday, we have been building AI-native internal platforms that allow users to interact with complex enterprise systems through copilots, agents, APIs, retrieval, and workflow automation.
That work made me think deeply about the future of Talent Technology. Most HR systems require users to know where to go, what task to open, what data to enter, and which system owns the workflow. But the future is likely a more intelligent engagement layer across systems.
Task
My responsibility was to help shape architecture that was more than just a chat interface. It needed to support secure, reliable, governed orchestration across complex workflows.
Action
I thought about the architecture in layers:
User Experience Layer: chat, dashboards, workflow UI, cards, widgets. Orchestration Layer: routing, workflow state, approvals, escalation, human-in-the-loop. Agent Layer: retrieval, summarization, drafting, recommendations, safe execution. Integration/API Layer: Workday, ATS, learning, performance, feedback, compensation, internal apps. Data Layer: structured records, documents, metadata, analytics. Trust Layer: permissions, audit logs, source grounding, privacy, evaluation, observability.
The important judgment was that chat alone is not the strategy. Sometimes the best interface is conversational. Sometimes it is a dashboard. Sometimes it is a structured guided workflow. The right answer depends on the user and the task.
Result
This architecture helped us think beyond point solutions and toward a platform pattern that could support multiple workflows, tools, and user types.
Growth / Reflection
For Talent systems, this matters because the data is sensitive and the workflows are human. Recruiting, compensation, performance, feedback, learning, and internal mobility all require trust, permissions, and accountability.
Signal Areas
Scope: Multi-layer platform architecture. Ambiguity: Future-state architecture without one obvious answer. Communication: Explained complex architecture simply. Leadership: Set direction beyond feature delivery. Ownership: Thought through trust, data, and workflow implications.
Strong Closing Line
“The engagement layer is not a prettier front end. It is governed orchestration over Talent workflows.”

Story 10: Business-Centric AI Adoption in Talent
Use For
GenAI in HR/Talent, business impact, judgment, responsible AI, Talent domain depth.
Situation
In both my Cornerstone and Workday experience, I have seen how Talent workflows create friction for employees, managers, recruiters, and HR teams. People struggle to find policies, write useful feedback, understand processes, summarize information, prepare for performance conversations, or navigate complex systems.
With GenAI, there is a real opportunity to improve those experiences. But Talent data is sensitive, so AI adoption has to be thoughtful.
Task
If I were applying AI to Talent Engineering, my responsibility would be to identify high-value use cases while making sure humans remain accountable for sensitive decisions.
Action
I would start with high-friction, high-volume, low-regret workflows:
Helping managers draft clearer feedback. Summarizing feedback themes with source references. Helping recruiters prepare candidate summaries. Helping employees find policies or learning resources. Helping Talent Ops reduce repetitive questions. Helping managers understand team workflows, onboarding tasks, or performance process steps.
I would be careful with autonomous decisions around hiring, compensation, performance ratings, employee relations, or promotion outcomes. In those areas, AI should support human judgment, not replace it.
I would also require access controls, auditability, source grounding, evaluation, and human approval for higher-risk actions.
Result
The business value is better productivity, better decision support, and reduced friction without creating unacceptable risk.
Growth / Reflection
The lesson is that the question should not be, “Where can we add AI?” The better question is, “Where can AI improve speed, clarity, scale, or decision quality while preserving trust?”
Signal Areas
Judgment: Balanced opportunity with risk. Talent Domain: Understood recruiting, feedback, learning, performance, and Talent Ops. Communication: Framed AI in business terms. Leadership: Defined safe adoption principles. Ambiguity: Applied AI to sensitive workflows carefully. Ownership: Took accountability for trust and governance.
Strong Closing Line
“In Talent workflows, AI should improve the quality of human judgment before it tries to replace judgment.”
