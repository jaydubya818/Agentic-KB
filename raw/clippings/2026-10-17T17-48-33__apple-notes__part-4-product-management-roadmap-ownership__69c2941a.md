PART 4 — PRODUCT MANAGEMENT & ROADMAP OWNERSHIP

15. HOW WOULD YOU BUILD THE PEOPLE SYSTEMS ROADMAP FROM SCRATCH?

Likelihood: EXTREMELY HIGH | 60–90 seconds

I’d begin with discovery rather than immediately building features.

First, meet with People Operations, Talent Acquisition, Payroll, Finance, IT, Security, and business leaders.

I’d understand their workflows, pain points, operational risks, and growth requirements.

Second, assess the current technology portfolio, including Workday, Greenhouse, integrations, reporting, and manual processes.

Third, establish a baseline of operational metrics.

Then I’d prioritize initiatives based on:

Business impact, employee experience, risk reduction, implementation effort, and scalability.

I’d organize the roadmap into three categories.

Foundation: Core HCM architecture, data quality, security, and integration reliability.

Optimization: Workflow improvements, self-service, automation, and reporting.

Innovation: AI-native employee experiences and agentic workflows.

I’d establish a quarterly roadmap with clear ownership, measurable outcomes, and regular stakeholder reviews.

The roadmap should explain not just what we’re building, but why it matters to the business.

16. HOW DO YOU PRIORITIZE COMPETING REQUESTS FROM HR LEADERS?

Likelihood: VERY HIGH | 60 seconds

I’d establish a transparent prioritization framework based on business value, employee impact, compliance risk, urgency, and engineering effort.

For example, if Payroll needs a critical compliance change while Talent Acquisition wants a new recruiting dashboard, I’d evaluate the operational risk and consequences of delaying each initiative.

A compliance issue affecting employee pay would typically take priority over a reporting enhancement.

I’d also distinguish between mandatory work, operational improvements, and strategic investments.

Then I’d communicate the trade-offs clearly to stakeholders.

At Workday, I own product prioritization across a shared AI platform supporting 300+ teams, so balancing competing requests and limited engineering capacity is something I deal with regularly.

My goal is to make prioritization objective, transparent, and aligned with business outcomes.

17. WHEN WOULD YOU CONFIGURE WORKDAY, BUY A PRODUCT, OR BUILD SOMETHING CUSTOM?

Likelihood: EXTREMELY HIGH | 60 seconds

My default approach is configure before customize, and reuse before building.

First, determine whether Workday already supports the business requirement through standard functionality.

If it does, I’d configure the native capability.

If it doesn’t, I’d evaluate whether a supported integration, marketplace solution, or existing vendor product addresses the gap.

I’d consider building a custom solution when the requirement is strategically important, creates meaningful differentiation, or cannot be met effectively through existing platforms.

I’d evaluate total cost of ownership, security, maintenance, scalability, integration complexity, and vendor dependency.

For example, if we need a better employee self-service experience, I wouldn’t immediately replace Workday functionality.

I’d consider whether we could build an AI-powered conversational layer that securely interacts with Workday through approved APIs.
