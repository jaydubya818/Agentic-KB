2. Detailed Recruiter Evaluation Criteria

Category 1: First Impression & Role Alignment

Weight: 10 points

A recruiter should understand within approximately 10 seconds why your background is relevant.

Evaluation criteria:

Professional headline matches the role’s actual responsibilities.
Current experience directly relates to the position.
Seniority and management scope are appropriate.
Required qualifications are immediately visible.
Resume establishes a clear professional identity.
Relevant experience appears before secondary experience.
No obvious contradictions between job requirements and background.

Full score: A recruiter can immediately identify why you qualify.

Category 2: Professional Summary

Weight: 5 points

Evaluation criteria:

Clearly establishes years of relevant experience.
Identifies primary technical and leadership strengths.
Demonstrates current engineering responsibility.
Highlights organizational scale.
Connects experience to the company’s needs.
Uses natural, credible language.
Avoids generic leadership statements and excessive keywords.

Full score: A concise, compelling summary that sounds authentic.

Category 3: Leadership Impact & Business Outcomes

Weight: 10 points

Evaluation criteria:

Measurable business outcomes.
Developer productivity improvements.
Engineering efficiency and delivery velocity.
Platform adoption and organizational scale.
Reliability, quality, and operational improvements.
Cost reductions.
Product adoption or revenue contribution.
Clear attribution of personal and team contributions.
Metrics that can be explained and defended.

Full score: Five or six compelling accomplishments demonstrating meaningful impact.

Category 4: Technical Depth & Engineering Credibility

Weight: 15 points

This is particularly important for technical engineering management positions.

Evaluation criteria:

Systems design and architecture experience.
Distributed systems knowledge.
Backend and full-stack engineering.
Technical tradeoff decisions.
Complex engineering problem-solving.
Production infrastructure.
Performance optimization.
Reliability engineering.
Security and access controls.
Technical implementation ownership.
Experience building reusable frameworks or abstractions.
Ability to evaluate and challenge Staff-level technical decisions.

Full score: Strong evidence of technical judgment and substantial engineering experience.

Category 5: Engineering Leadership & People Management

Weight: 15 points

This category evaluates actual management experience, not simply team size.

Evaluation criteria:

Recruiting and hiring decisions.

Compensation decisions.
Managing underperformance.

Organizational design.
Retention and engagement.
Conflict resolution.
Developing future technical leaders.

Full score: Demonstrated experience independently managing the complete engineering employee lifecycle.

5. Critical Requirements Override the Score

A 95/100 resume should not automatically be considered application-ready.

Some job requirements are nonnegotiable.

For example, a company might explicitly require prior Staff-level engineering experience, a particular location, or direct experience managing production infrastructure.

If a candidate lacks a required qualification, a high overall score cannot compensate for it.

For every future application, we should separately identify:

Required qualifications: Must be clearly demonstrated.

Preferred qualifications: Strengthen competitiveness but aren’t mandatory.

Differentiators: Experience that makes you stand out from similarly qualified candidates.

⸻

6. My Recommendation for Future Resume Reviews

I recommend using this same framework consistently, with the role-specific criteria adjusted for each job description.

Every review should produce four outputs:

1. Weighted score out of 100: How well the resume demonstrates the requirements.

2. Critical qualification assessment: Whether any mandatory requirements are missing or unclear.

3. Evidence gaps: Where the resume makes claims without enough supporting detail.

4. Highest-impact improvements: The specific changes most likely to strengthen the application.

The Standard I’d Use Going Forward

95+ should mean the resume provides exceptional evidence of fit, not simply that we’ve rewritten it until the score reaches 95.

I would also evaluate the resume from two distinct perspectives:

Recruiter: Is this candidate worth advancing?
Hiring manager: Is there convincing evidence this person can succeed in the role?

That distinction is important.
