Product Management 

Role in one sentence: Treat Workday and the connected People Systems as an internal product portfolio: understand user needs, decide what to improve, deliver reliable solutions, and measure business outcomes.
Six priorities: Discovery · Roadmap · Prioritization · Workflow Design · Delivery · Measurement
Core message: “I connect People team needs with technical delivery, balancing employee experience, operational efficiency, security, and global scalability.”

1. WHAT PRODUCT OWNERSHIP MEANS HERE
￼
Key distinction: Product management decides which problems to solve and why. Project management organizes how and when the work gets delivered. This role needs both.

2. PRODUCT STRATEGY AND ROADMAP VOCABULARY
￼
Roadmap answer:
“I’d build the roadmap around business outcomes: reliable employee data, faster onboarding, efficient People operations, and support for global growth. I’d use user feedback, operational data, risk, and delivery effort to sequence the work.”

3. DISCOVERY — UNDERSTAND THE PROBLEM
￼
Nscale users to consider: Employees · Managers · Recruiters · People Operations · HR partners · Payroll · Finance · IT · Security.
Discovery answer:
“I’d speak with the people doing the work, observe the current process, and review support tickets and transaction data. Then I’d define the problem, establish a baseline, and agree on what successful improvement would look like.”
Example problem statement:
“Recruiters and People Operations repeatedly correct new-hire records because required fields and organizational values do not align between systems. That creates manual work and delays onboarding.”

4. PRIORITIZATION — WHAT GETS BUILT FIRST
￼
Practical decision order:
Address critical operational failures and required deadlines.
Assess business impact and affected users.
Evaluate dependencies, effort, and ongoing support.
Explain what moves forward and what is deferred.
Revisit decisions when evidence changes.
Prioritization answer:
“I’d make the criteria visible: business impact, urgency, risk, affected users, effort, and dependencies. Critical payroll or access issues may take priority over convenience features. I’d explain the trade-offs and confirm the decision with the relevant stakeholders.”
Remember: A scoring framework supports judgment; it does not replace it.

5. REQUIREMENTS AND WORKFLOW DESIGN
￼
Example user story:
“As a People Operations specialist, I want validated hire data to transfer from Greenhouse into Workday so that I can onboard employees without re-entering information.”
Example acceptance criteria:
Required fields are validated.
The correct organization and position are mapped.
Retries do not create duplicate records.
Failures reach a named owner.
Authorized users can reconcile the result.
Sensitive information remains appropriately restricted.
Workflow answer:
“I’d design the full journey, including handoffs, ownership, exceptions, and downstream effects. A successful integration still needs a usable process for the people operating it.”

6. DELIVERY, RELEASES, AND ADOPTION
￼
Delivery answer:
“I’d deliver in manageable increments with clear acceptance criteria and business validation. Before launch, I’d confirm support ownership, communications, training, and recovery procedures. After launch, I’d review adoption and operational results.”
Remember: Technical deployment is one milestone. Successful adoption and improved outcomes establish value.

7. METRICS — HOW YOU KNOW IT WORKED
￼
￼
Measurement answer:
“I’d establish the baseline before implementation and agree on the intended outcome. For onboarding, I’d track completion time, manual corrections, and employee readiness, alongside security and reliability measures.”

8. STAKEHOLDERS AND PLATFORM INVESTMENT
￼
When stakeholders disagree:
“I’d bring the discussion back to the shared objective and supporting evidence. I’d make the constraints and trade-offs explicit, recommend a path, and confirm who owns the decision.”
When deciding where a capability belongs:
“I’d evaluate user fit, data ownership, integration complexity, security, cost, and maintainability. The recommendation should support the full portfolio and the team’s ability to operate it.”

9. AI PRODUCT MANAGEMENT
￼
AI answer:
“I’d start with a measurable use case, such as answering policy questions or triaging onboarding exceptions. I’d validate the data and permissions, evaluate quality and cost, pilot with a limited group, and expand based on demonstrated value.”

10. FIRST 90 DAYS — PROPOSED APPROACH
￼
Five prompts to keep beside you:
￼
Questions to ask Nscale:
“Which People workflows create the most friction today?”
“What roadmap commitments already exist?”
“How are priorities decided between People, Corporate Engineering, and other stakeholders?”
“How is time divided between product ownership and hands-on configuration?”
“What measurable outcomes would make the first six months successful?”
