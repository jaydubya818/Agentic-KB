AI, Agents, Copilots, and Assistants in Talent Tech

 Master Positioning

“I would start with workflow pain, not the technology.”

In Talent Tech, I would look for places where employees, managers, recruiters, and Talent partners are losing time, context, confidence, or clarity.

AI can create real value in areas like:

Manager enablement
Talent mobility
Skills intelligence
Recruiting coordination
Employee and manager self-service
Talent Operations automation
Learning and career growth
Talent analytics
Policy and process navigation

But for Talent Tech specifically, AI should improve human judgment, not replace it.

The principle I would use is:

“Decision support, never unreviewed decision replacement — especially in Talent workflows.”


AI-native Talent Intelligence Layer.

One AI opportunity I’d be excited to explore is an AI-native Talent Intelligence Layer. I would not think of it as just adding a chatbot on top of HR systems. I would think of it as a trusted intelligence and action layer across the Talent ecosystem.

Talent systems are often fragmented across Workday HCM as the source of truth, recruiting platforms, feedback tools, compensation workflows, learning systems, internal mobility platforms, analytics, and first-party applications. The user should not need to know which system owns which data or workflow.

A manager should be able to ask, “What should I pay attention to across my team this week?” and get a role-aware summary of hiring risks, onboarding needs, feedback gaps, skill gaps, mobility opportunities, org changes, and recommended actions.

The key design principle is trust. The system should be permission-aware, source-grounded, auditable, and built to support human judgment — not replace it. For sensitive areas like hiring, compensation, performance, and feedback, AI should provide context and recommendations, but humans remain accountable for decisions.

Longer term, I would design it as a closed-loop, semi-automonous Talent system: it senses what is happening, reasons over the context, recommends or executes approved actions, validates outcomes, and learns from what worked or failed.

That is where I think Talent Tech is heading: from systems of record, to systems of intelligence, to systems of workforce action.

Key capabilities I would think about include:

Role-aware Talent Assistant
    A single front door for employees, managers, recruiters, HRBPs, and Talent leaders. The experience would change based on the user’s role, permissions, team, and business context.
Manager Weekly Digest
    A proactive summary of what changed across a manager’s team, including hiring pipeline risks, onboarding status, feedback gaps, internal mobility opportunities, skill gaps, org changes, and recommended actions.
Recruiting Intelligence Agent
    A capability that summarizes candidate pipeline health, interview loop blockers, candidate feedback themes, hiring manager follow-ups, offer risks, and next-best actions.
Feedback Intelligence Agent
    A tool that helps improve the clarity and actionability of feedback, summarizes feedback themes, and helps managers identify where feedback is stale, vague, or missing. It should never invent feedback; it should improve and synthesize human-provided input.
