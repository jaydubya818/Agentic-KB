Story Bank

1. CORNERSTONE ATS / RECRUITING CLOUD BUILD

Best for: Building from a blank canvas · Roadmap ownership · Implementation · Recruiting integrations

SITUATION:
“At Cornerstone, we were building an enterprise recruiting platform from the ground up. We had fewer than 24 months, more than 100 pages of evolving requirements, customer commitments, and complex integration dependencies.”

TASK:
“My responsibility was to help turn that ambiguity into a focused delivery plan and a complete recruiting experience customers could actually adopt.”

ACTION:
“I partnered with Product and business stakeholders to separate core workflows, contractual commitments, integration dependencies, and later enhancements.

We prioritized the complete journey—from requisition and approvals through interviews, offers, and the onboarding handoff—over a larger collection of disconnected features.

I helped scale the organization to approximately 24 engineers, establish ownership and milestones, and define automation, environment, and release-readiness expectations.”

RESULT:
“We delivered Recruiting Cloud in 23 months and 28 days. The product earned industry recognition, including a Top HR Product award and a Gold award for Best New Human Capital Management Solution.”

CONNECTION TO NSCALE:
“I’d bring that same discipline to Nscale’s People Systems portfolio: define the employee outcome, establish the minimum complete workflow, sequence dependencies, and make ownership clear.”

2. OPERATION STABILIZE — TALENT AND HCM PORTFOLIO

Best for: Platform stability · Portfolio prioritization · Integrations · Data integrity · Stakeholder trust

SITUATION:
“At Cornerstone, we had approximately 21 interconnected Talent and HCM products with different architectures, integration patterns, and quality standards. Customers expected a connected employee lifecycle, but problems often appeared at the handoffs between systems.”

TASK:
“I needed to help improve end-to-end reliability and establish a consistent way to prioritize and resolve issues across teams.”

ACTION:
“I organized priorities around customer impact, workflow criticality, data integrity, permissions, and integration risk, rather than defect count alone.

We introduced cross-product workflow validation, expanded automation around critical journeys, clarified ownership, and established shared release-readiness criteria.

I also made dependencies, unresolved risks, and decisions visible to executives. Where necessary, we deferred features to address systemic reliability problems.”

RESULT:
“We improved stability and release confidence, reduced escalation pressure, and established clearer accountability across the portfolio.”

CONNECTION TO NSCALE:
“For Nscale, I’d apply that approach to Greenhouse-to-Workday handoffs and downstream employee workflows, validating data, permissions, exceptions, and ownership across the full process.”

3. AI Agent Factory PLATFORM - 

Best for: Practical AI · Product strategy · Build-versus-buy decisions · Governance · Scaling through shared capabilities

SITUATION:
“At Workday, demand for AI was growing across teams building agents, knowledge assistants, and workflow automation. Without a shared foundation, teams would repeatedly build their own retrieval, integrations, evaluations, security, and observability.”

TASK:
“I led a 12-engineer organization helping turn that demand into a reusable platform and a practical delivery model.”

ACTION:
“We built Agent Factory  for governed agent execution and Hopper for enterprise knowledge retrieval.

I helped prioritize the shared capabilities teams needed most, aligned Engineering, Product, and Security, and established expectations for permissions, evaluations, monitoring, and production readiness.

We centralized repeated platform capabilities while leaving domain teams responsible for their specific business workflows.”

RESULT:
“The platforms supported an engineering organization of 300-plus scrum teams and approximately 3,000 engineers, enabling 25-plus customer-facing agents and 50-plus internal agents.”

CONNECTION TO NSCALE:
“At Nscale, I’d start with a specific employee problem, evaluate delivered Workday capabilities first, and use integrations or Extend AI where there is a clear gap. I’d measure the outcome through resolution time, completion rates, quality, and cost.”



4. AGENT STABILIZATION — A RELEASE THAT DID NOT LAND

Best for: Failure · Accountability · Release management · Testing · Balancing speed and reliability

SITUATION:
“At Workday, we moved quickly to release our first Agent capability, but our staging validation and operational readiness were not strong enough. Defects reached users and affected confidence in the agent platform.”

￼

TASK:
“My responsibility was to contain the impact, restore confidence, and correct the release process. I owned the gap rather than placing blame on the engineer closest to the change.”

ACTION:
“We disabled the unstable capability and temporarily reduced roadmap expansion.

I aligned Product and Engineering around stronger staging validation, critical-workflow testing, observability, named defect ownership, and release evidence.

I explained the tradeoff clearly: accept a short-term delay to establish a reliable foundation for future delivery.”

RESULT:
“We established a stronger release process, improved platform stability, and made readiness decisions based on validation evidence and operational ownership.”

CONNECTION TO NSCALE:
“I’d apply that lesson to Workday changes by validating business processes, security, tenant differences, and downstream integrations before production. A change is complete when the workflow is validated and supportable.”
