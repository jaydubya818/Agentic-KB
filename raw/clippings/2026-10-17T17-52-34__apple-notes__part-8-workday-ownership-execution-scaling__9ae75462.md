PART 8 — WORKDAY OWNERSHIP, EXECUTION & SCALING

38. HOW WOULD YOU SCALE WORKDAY FROM 1,000 EMPLOYEES TO 10,000?

Likelihood: EXTREMELY HIGH | 90 seconds

I’d focus on building a scalable foundation now, rather than introducing complexity that becomes expensive to correct later.

First, I’d evaluate the Workday organizational architecture, including supervisory organizations, companies, locations, job profiles, staffing models, and security.

Second, I’d standardize Core HCM business processes, including hiring, onboarding, employee changes, transfers, compensation changes, and terminations.

Third, I’d establish reliable integrations connecting Greenhouse, payroll, identity management, finance, and other People Systems.

Fourth, I’d design for global expansion, accounting for country-specific requirements, localization, data privacy, and regulatory compliance.

Fifth, I’d implement automated regression testing, integration monitoring, data reconciliation, and release management.

Finally, I’d establish a People Systems product roadmap that evolves with organizational growth.

At Cornerstone, I joined when the company was relatively small and helped support its expansion to approximately 6,000 employees.

I also led engineering across 21 HCM and Talent products and more than 50 integrations.

That experience taught me the importance of standardization, integration reliability, and clear operational ownership.

KEY MESSAGE: Build the architecture, governance, and operational controls for 10,000 employees while keeping the experience simple enough for 1,000.

39. WHAT WOULD YOU DO IF YOU INHERITED A WORKDAY TENANT WITH POOR CONFIGURATION AND UNRELIABLE INTEGRATIONS?

Likelihood: VERY HIGH | 60–90 seconds

I’d begin with a structured assessment rather than immediately changing configurations.

First, I’d review the current tenant architecture, business processes, security model, integrations, and reporting.

Second, I’d examine operational evidence, including integration failures, support tickets, data discrepancies, and recurring production incidents.

Third, I’d identify the highest-risk areas affecting employees and business operations.

Payroll accuracy, employee access, and critical worker transactions would receive immediate attention.

Fourth, I’d establish a remediation roadmap focused on stabilization, standardization, automation, and long-term improvements.

I’d also introduce monitoring, automated regression testing, and clearer ownership across integrations.

At Cornerstone, I led a 90-day stabilization initiative across 21 products and more than 50 integrations.

We established clear ownership, escalation paths, and release controls across Product, Engineering, Quality, Support, and Operations.

I’d apply that same structured approach at Nscale.

KEY MESSAGE: Stabilize critical operations first, identify systemic problems, and build a sustainable improvement roadmap.
