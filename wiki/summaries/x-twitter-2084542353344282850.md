## TL;DR
The tweet lists 18 project types meant to prove production AI engineering skill, spanning RAG, cost routing, multi-agent research, eval harnesses, observability, guardrails, local-first development, copilot UI, fine-tuning, multi-tenancy, CI/CD, vector databases, memory, inference serving, HITL workflows, automation pipelines, domain benchmarks, and open-source contribution.

## Key Points
1. The source frames production AI engineering as systems that demonstrate shipping capability, not isolated model prompts.
2. Retrieval quality appears as a first-class project: PDF Q&A with page citations, hybrid search, reranking, and grounding.
3. Operational discipline appears repeatedly: automated evals before deploy, observability dashboards, CI/CD, canaries, rollback, rate limits, and audit trails.
4. The checklist treats security and governance as product requirements: prompt-injection detection, PII redaction, sandboxing, tenant isolation, human approval, and audit trails.
5. The tool stacks are illustrative rather than prescriptive: examples include LangGraph, SQLite-vec, LiteLLM, DeepEval, RAGAS, LangSmith, OpenTelemetry, Guardrails AI, Ollama, LanceDB, Vercel AI SDK, PEFT, Supabase, Stripe, ArgoCD, Qdrant/Weaviate, Redis, vLLM/SGLang, FastAPI, Celery, and pytest.
